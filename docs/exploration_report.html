Improving the DOF ladder and the rings

A cluster sweep over the training design, scoring two objectives at once: does the learned distance's volume-growth dimension read the true degrees of freedom (the ladder), and do the component embeddings form clean rings (isometry). The exploration converged on a single, clear answer.

the two objectives

What we were optimizing

  • The ladder. At a state where a key frees 1+m of five agents, the true local dimension is 1+m. We want the learned distance's VGT volume-growth dimension to track that ladder from 1 to 5. Scored by mean-abs-error, correlation, and monotonicity.
  • The rings. Each agent's positions should embed as a clean ring (isometric to its cycle). Scored projection-free by the fraction of positions whose nearest neighbour is graph-adjacent (nn_adj), and by the isometry ratio ed(6)/ed(1).

Every configuration was trained from local moves only (multi-scale isometry plus repulsion), then both objectives were measured by a self-contained worker. Configs were fanned out across the cluster as Slurm array jobs.

the key finding

Rings are bought with training time

Across the whole grid, no architectural knob pushed the rings past ~0.72 at a fixed 8k steps. The one lever that mattered was how long we trained. Ring order climbs and saturates near-perfect; the isometry ratio keeps rising more slowly.

The ladder is the easy objective: robust everywhere as long as lam_iso stays low and K=6. The rings are the hard one, and only training time moves them meaningfully. The architectural knobs (d, G, high lam) mildly trade off the two, so they are not the way to improve either.

the interaction

The two objectives are compatible

They co-improve with training, which is the clean win: more steps buy better rings without breaking the ladder. The only tension is minor: as isometry strengthens, the single-agent stratum reads slightly above 1 (DOF-1 drifts 1.85 → 2.36), nudging ladder-error up while the ladder stays perfectly monotone and separated. That is a correlation-dimension quirk on a now-cleanly-curved 1-D ring, not a regression.

Why the earlier runs looked like "not rings" Nothing was fundamentally wrong. The embeddings looked scrambled only because of under-training (8–12k steps) on top of two prerequisites that had to be fixed first: the gate must not see the displacement, and the repel margin must be low. With those in place and enough steps, the rings appear on their own.

the conclusion

The recipe

No-displacement gate, low repel margin (~5), lam_iso ~3, K=6, d=48, G=48, trained 32k–48k steps.

This yields a clean monotone VGT ladder and near-perfect rings (nn_adj ~0.95–0.98) together. Rings scale with training and saturate by ~32k (diminishing returns beyond); the ladder needs only that lam_iso stay low and K=6. Both fixes that unlocked this were architectural prerequisites (gate, margin) plus training time, not a delicate balance of hyperparameters.

Open edge The isometry scale (ratio ~2.2 at 48k vs an ideal 6) is still climbing. The ring topology is essentially solved; a fully-stretched isometric metric would need much longer training or an explicit scale constraint. For reading dimension, the topology is what matters, so this is a refinement, not a blocker.
