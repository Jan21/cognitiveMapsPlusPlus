Switchyard: a grid world where the factors need each other

A worker, a crate, levers, gates, a pressure plate. Every element is borrowed from a published benchmark; the new thing is that they are wired into one dependency web, so the distance between two configurations is a genuinely joint quantity. Built as the comparison stage for goal-conditioned distance models.

About the images: every picture on this page is an illustrative schematic drawn for this document (hand-authored SVG), not a screenshot of the environment. Layouts are simplified; real maps are randomly generated 7x7 yards.

The game

A four-room yard. The worker moves in four directions and can push the crate (never pull: pushes into corners are forever). Room passages are gates, open or closed according to gate bits. Standing on a lever and pulling it flips a wired subset of gates at once. While the crate sits on the pressure plate, the plate's wired gates are forced open regardless of their bits. Some cells are one-way chutes. A task is: from this full configuration (worker, crate, gate bits), reach that one, in the fewest moves.

Why the distance is a joint quantity

Because of these cycles, the shortest path often parks the crate on the plate purely to hold gates open, detours the worker through lever rooms in an order dictated by the wiring, and only then routes toward the goal. No sum of per-factor distances reproduces this.

State is the factored vector [worker_cell, crate_cell, gate_bits]; the map's wall layout and wiring form the configuration (the analogue of the coupling world's mobility/link keys). Supervision and evaluation mirror our existing protocol exactly: pairs (state, goal, BFS distance), smooth L1, MAE and correlation on held-out configurations.

Probe: distances really do not factorize

For each sampled pair we compare the true BFS distance against the best factorized approximation: independent worker walk (all gates open, crate ghosted) + independent crate distance + minimal lever pulls, summed. If this proxy tracked the truth, the benchmark would be pointless. It does not:

The pure-Sokoban control matters: pushing alone already defeats the proxy somewhat (corr 0.66), but only the gate/lever/plate wiring makes distances depend on the configuration at fixed endpoints. That wiring sensitivity is the benchmark's defining interdependence metric.

First experimental results

Two models, identical encoder and training (smooth L1 on distance pairs from 40 maps), evaluated on entirely unseen maps and wirings; run on a V100 (dgx-ciirc), 20k steps, d=128, T=14.

recall-flow integrator (accumulated-displacement readout) scalar head (same encoder)

The scale trend is the headline. At toy scale (12 maps, small model, 6k steps) the integrator led the scalar head by 0.27 MAE; at full scale (40 maps, d=128, 20k steps) the lead grew to 0.94 MAE and +0.12 correlation. The benchmark separates the methods in exactly the direction the interdependence thesis predicts, and with test MAE still ~3.5 at mean distance 24, it is nowhere near saturated: room for the full baseline comparison (IQE, MRNFixed, symmetric norm via torchqmet) to be informative.
