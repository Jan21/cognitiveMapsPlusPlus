Switchyard campaign: results so far

Interim report, 2026-08-14 morning. One model (the recall-flow integrator, accumulated-displacement readout) against one baseline (a scalar-head regressor on the SAME structural encoder), across five experiment families on five machines (~30 runs; 22 reported). Metric everywhere: test MAE / correlation on held-out data, distances 1-24, mean sampled distance ~21-24. Environment and design: switchyard.html, switchyard_design.md.

Three findings so far. (1) The integrator's advantage over the scalar head grows monotonically with the amount of factor interdependence, from a dead heat on a plain maze (margin 0.05) to a 1.2-1.4 MAE gap on the full environment; it is specifically an interdependence advantage, not general capacity. (2) The integrator does not break anywhere on the current ladder: it plateaus at ~2.0 MAE from mid-complexity upward while the baseline keeps degrading; the environment finds the baseline's ceiling, not yet the integrator's. (3) Both generalization axes (unseen wirings, unseen layouts) improve steadily with map count and converge to the same ~1.8-1.9 plateau at 800 maps, suggesting remaining error is fit, not transfer.

Curriculum helps both models but asymmetrically: it partially rescues the scalar head (which needs the easy phases to find the coupling structure at all) yet leaves a 0.96 gap. The integrator gains only modestly, consistent with the claim that its architecture, not the training schedule, carries the interdependence. Curriculum seed 1 is running.

E3: length generalization

No results yet. Five runs (train on distances ≤8 or ≤12, evaluate to 24, with and without the global-base fix) are training on dgx-osu after a CUDA-wheel mishap cost the first night (torch cu130 silently fell back to CPU; reinstalled cu121 and relaunched). Expectation from the coupling-world experiments: without the fix, beyond-range predictions saturate; the interesting question is whether --globalbase restores extrapolation here as it did there.

Protocol

  • Models: recall-flow integrator (T=14 weight-shared steps, accumulated token-displacement readout) vs scalar-head regressor; both on the same structural encoder (per-gate/lever/plate/chute/wall tokens composed from shared embeddings, so unseen maps and wirings are new combinations of known pieces), d=128, 4 layers, 40k steps, smooth L1, identical pools.
  • Environment: 7x7 yard, 3 gates, 2 levers, 1 plate, 1 chute; ~13K joint states per map; exact BFS ground truth; distances to 24.
  • Splits: unseen layouts (map), unseen wirings (wire), capped training distance (Rtrain). Pools bucket-sampled per distance.
  • Fleet: dgx-ciirc (V100s), ciirc-old-cluster (A40s), dgx-osu (A100s), pc-phd1/5 (4060 Ti). ~30 runs total, 22 reported.
