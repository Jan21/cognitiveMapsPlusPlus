Discrete grid benchmarks for goal-conditioned distance functions

Where the goal-conditioned value-function literature tests on discrete, grid-like worlds. Compiled 2026-08-13 from the papers in our prior-work and baselines surveys; every environment verified against the paper PDF and repository. Continuous benchmarks (Fetch, AntMaze, OGBench locomotion) are excluded; the exclusion list at the bottom says what was checked. Companion text version: grid_benchmarks.md.

About the images. Every picture on this page is an illustrative schematic drawn for this document (hand-authored SVG), not a screenshot from the benchmark. Each caption names the paper figure or source the drawing is based on. Layouts are simplified and not to scale; grid sizes in the drawings are reduced for legibility, with true sizes given in the facts list.

Tier 1: benchmarks from the goal-conditioned distance literature itself

One-way-doors gridworld

PQE (Wang & Isola, ICLR 2022) · IQE (NeurReps 2022), identical rerun
  • World: gym-minigrid, roughly 31x31 cells; central room + 3 side rooms per flank; ~9 one-way doors (no-op from the blocked side); 4 cardinal actions.
  • Protocol: offline Q-learning on epsilon-greedy data (eps 0.6, gamma 0.95); eval = greedy 1-step planning, 50 start/goal pairs.
  • Ground truth: directed BFS, exact.
  • Code: not released; reimplement from App. D.2.3 (~1 day with minigrid).
Why for us: the baselines' own discrete benchmark; one-way doors are rule-induced distance, same flavor as our knob/link gating. The reviewer-proof choice.

KeyDoorGridWorld

MAD: Learning the Minimum Action Distance (2025)
  • World: 13x13; state (x, y, key-flag); deterministic cardinal actions; key pickup irreversible; door passable only with key. Distances asymmetric.
  • Siblings in the same paper: CliffWalking 4x12 (cliff teleports to start without reset, an asymmetric shortcut) and NoisyGridWorld 13x13 (Gaussian noise dims + 50% action noise).
  • Ground truth: BFS exact; paper evaluates against exact all-pairs distances.
  • Code: public, github.com/lorenzosteccanella/MinimumActionDistance.
Why for us: closest published mechanic to our coupling world (a gate that opens only after visiting a state); runnable in hours.

4-Room / Maze value fitting (with wind asymmetry)

DeepNorm / WideNorm (Pitis et al., ICLR 2020)
  • World: 11x11 grids, 4 cardinal actions, state/goal as 11x11x3 one-hot planes; UVFA-style V(s,g) trained by TD, gamma 1; held-out-goal and held-out-state splits; success + SPL metrics.
  • Asymmetry: optional wind cost on west/south moves (Dijkstra ground truth); symmetric variant BFS-exact.
  • Same paper, larger graphs: 50x50x50 wraparound grid (+2 directed prunings), 25x25 taxi, 25x25 pusher with irreversible pushes (125K-391K states, landmark-feature distance regression).
  • Code: github.com/spitis/deepnorms covers the graph suite (Make3dAndTaxi.ipynb); the gridworld value-fitting code is not in the repo.
Why for us: the closest published evaluation protocol to ours (supervised value fitting with held-out goals/states on a small grid).

MiniGrid DoorKey + Hypermaze

MetricRL (arXiv:2402.10820), distance-based value V = γd
  • DoorKey: two rooms split by a wall; state = agent pos + key pos + door flag; translations + pickup + open-door actions; also an 80x80 image variant. Empty: open grid, rotations removed.
  • Hypermaze: n-dimensional S-maze; dimension sweep 2-5, cells/dim 10-50: a discrete difficulty ladder adjacent to our DOF story.
  • Ground truth: BFS exact (deterministic discrete dynamics).
  • Code: no official repo; MiniGrid base is public (pip minigrid, MIT, maintained).
Why for us: published precedent for distance-based goal-conditioned values on MiniGrid; the standard package reviewers recognize.

UVFA grids: 4-rooms + LavaWorld

Universal Value Function Approximators (Schaul et al., ICML 2015)
  • 4-rooms: classical grid, 4 cardinal actions; UVFA trained on goals in 3 rooms, tested on the unseen 4th room (the original goal-extrapolation test).
  • LavaWorld: 7x7 rooms (1-3 rooms; one 10x10), deadly lava cells, teleporting doors, per-cell binary-plane observations of the current room only.
  • Ground truth: BFS exact (lava = absorbing, doors = teleport edges).
  • Code: none official; small reimplementation.
Why for us: the founding paper's own testbeds; historically legible and nearly free to add.

Horizon Generalization tabular mazes

Myers, Ji, Eysenbach (ICLR 2025), Appendix F
  • World: deterministic maze with walls; 5 actions (4 cardinal + no-op); random-trajectory offline data; in one setting only 14% of start-goal pairs are connected in training.
  • Ground truth: the paper itself uses Dijkstra; evaluation binned by goal distance (10-50 steps): the published discrete version of our train-short/test-long axis.
  • Code: repo exists but ships only the continuous environments; the tabular mazes need reimplementation from Appendix F.
Why for us: precedent and protocol for distance-binned length-generalization evaluation on grids.

Tier 2: discrete mazes from the planning literature (best tooling, different supervision)

VIN gridworlds

Value Iteration Networks (Tamar et al., NeurIPS 2016)
  • World: random obstacle grids; input = obstacle map + goal map as images; labels from exact shortest paths; plus a Mars-rover 16x16 variant from real terrain.
  • Tooling: datasets downloadable (81K-1.5M samples); github.com/kentsommer/pytorch-value-iteration-networks (maintained port), original avivt/VIN.
  • Ground truth: BFS by construction.
Why for us: instant map-generalization testbed with zero dataset work.

GPPN mazes

Gated Path Planning Networks (Lee et al., ICML 2018)
  • World: DFS mazes + probabilistic wall pruning; three transition mechanisms, incl. orientation-dependent differential drive; a 3D ViZDoom render of the same mazes.
  • Tooling: generator + 12 pre-generated dataset configs, github.com/RLAgent/gated-path-planning-networks.
  • Ground truth: exact optimal lengths ship with the %Optimal metric.
Why for us: harder maze topology than VIN plus a state-space-structure knob (orientation), cheap to run.

easy-to-hard mazes

Schwarzschild et al. NeurIPS 2021 · Bansal et al. NeurIPS 2022 (Deep Thinking / recall)
  • World: unique-solution DFS mazes as images; target = shortest-path mask, so the distance is the mask length; image parses back to a grid for full BFS fields.
  • Tooling: pip install easy-to-hard-data; sizes 9-59 packaged (50K/10K per size); training code aks2203/deep-thinking.
  • Ground truth: exact by construction.
Why for us: the community's canonical train-small/test-huge maze stage: exactly where our budget-free length-extrapolation claim should be demonstrated once the global-base fix lands.

MiniGrid / FourRooms infrastructure

Farama MiniGrid · Sutton FourRooms lineage · Laplacian-RL rooms (ICLR 2019)
  • Package: pip minigrid (MIT, maintained): FourRooms, DoorKey, MultiRoom, KeyCorridor, ObstructedMaze; discrete actions; the full grid is programmatically accessible, so exact BFS over (cell, direction, inventory, door state) is easy.
  • GCRL usage: MetricRL (above); Laplacian representations for goal-reaching (OneRoom/TwoRooms/HardMaze, ICLR 2019); SR literature (Stachenfeld 2017 gridworld simulations, incl. the Tolman detour maze).
  • Also: HER's bit-flipping S = {0,1}^n (discrete hypercube, Hamming ground truth): one line of code, classic sanity check.
Why for us: the standard, maintained substrate if we want a recognizable off-the-shelf world rather than a paper-specific reimplementation.

Recommended comparison set

  1. PQE/IQE one-way-doors gridworld (reimplement, ~1 day): the baselines' own benchmark; one-way doors match our rule-gated distances.
  2. MAD KeyDoorGridWorld + CliffWalking (public code, hours): key-gated door is the closest published mechanic to our knob/link world, with asymmetric distances.
  3. DeepNorm 4-Room/Maze 11x11 with the wind variant (reimplement from App. F): the nearest published protocol (value fitting, held-out goals/states).
  4. easy-to-hard mazes (pip, hours): the standard stage for the length-extrapolation claim once globalbase is ported.
  5. MiniGrid DoorKey (pip, hours): recognizable standard package, MetricRL precedent.
