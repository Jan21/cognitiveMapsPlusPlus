# Accumulated latent increments for goal-conditioned distance learning

## User

Prior-work search: is a goal-conditioned value/distance function computed by ACCUMULATING PER-STEP LATENT INCREMENTS of an iterative network a novel idea, or has it been done?

CONTEXT (our method, described precisely so you can judge closeness):
We work in goal-conditioned reinforcement learning where the value function is a distance: conditioned on a goal state g, the value of state s is the estimated number of steps (BFS geodesic distance) from s to g. Our architecture computes this distance as follows. A shared encoder maps the current state and the goal state to sets of tokens. A single weight-shared transformer block is then applied for T recurrent steps; the goal and start tokens are re-injected into the sequence at every step (a "recall" mechanism). Crucially, the distance is NOT read out by a scalar regression head and NOT computed as a norm between two embeddings. Instead it is the accumulated path length of the latent trajectory itself: at each recurrent step we add the norms of the token displacements, cost += sum_i ||z_i(t+1) - z_i(t)||, and the final prediction is a learned positive scalar times this accumulated cost. In other words, the value is the integral of local latent "speed" along a learned iterative flow from the start representation toward the goal representation. Distinctive properties: (a) the number of steps T is fixed, yet the model extrapolates to distances far beyond the training range because the per-step increments carry the magnitude (no compute budget tied to distance); (b) training supervises only ground-truth shortest-path distances, in some settings only local/short-range pairs, and we evaluate extrapolation to longer distances and unseen environments; (c) no policy is learned, the network is a pure distance oracle.

MAIN QUESTION:
Has anyone previously proposed computing a goal-conditioned value or distance function as the sum/integral of per-iteration displacement norms (or other nonnegative local increments) of a recurrent or iterative network's hidden state? Find the closest prior work, whether an exact match, a partial anticipation, or a clearly related mechanism in a different domain.

## Deep Research Output

Novelty Search: Goal-Conditioned Distance as the Accumulated Sum of Per-Iteration Latent Displacement Norms
TL;DR
The specific mechanism you describe — computing a goal-conditioned BFS/geodesic distance as the accumulated sum of per-iteration latent-token displacement norms (cost += Σ_i ||z_i(t+1) − z_i(t)||) of a fixed-T, weight-shared recurrent transformer with start/goal "recall" re-injection, times a learned positive scalar — has no exact precedent in the literature I surveyed. It is best characterized as novel in its readout, assembling three well-established ingredients that have never been combined this way.
Every closely-related goal-conditioned distance method reads distance either as a norm/quasimetric between two static embeddings (VIP, Quasimetric RL/IQE/PQE/MRN, Contrastive RL, temporal-distance methods) or as a scalar regression head — precisely the two mechanisms your method disclaims. The "distance = integral of local latent speed along a curve" idea exists only in Riemannian latent-geometry work (pullback metrics/geodesics), where the path length is obtained by optimizing a curve, not by summing displacements of a forward recurrent pass, and is not goal-conditioned RL supervised on shortest paths.
The quantity Σ||Δz|| (arc length / kinetic energy / transport cost) appears repeatedly in the literature but always as a regularizer (Neural ODE / OT-Flow), a diagnostic probe (transformer trajectory-geometry papers), or an early-exit/halting signal (looped/recurrent-depth transformers) — never as the supervised prediction target. Your use of it as the output is the crux of the novelty.
Key Findings
No exact match. No prior work reads out a goal-conditioned value/distance as the accumulated per-step displacement norm of a recurrent network's hidden state. This is the central result.
The dominant paradigm is the opposite of yours: distance-as-embedding-norm. Quasimetric RL (QRL, Wang et al., ICML 2023) and its embedding backbones — Interval Quasimetric Embeddings (IQE), Poisson Quasimetric Embeddings (PQE), Metric Residual Networks (MRN, Liu et al. 2023), DeepNorm/WideNorm (Pitis et al., ICLR 2020) — all compute distance from a pair of static latents. IQE internally sums interval lengths across components, and MRN sums a symmetric + asymmetric term, but these are aggregations over embedding dimensions in a single shot, not accumulations over iterative compute steps. MRN's readout is literally a one-shot component-wise sum: V(s,g) = −‖φ(s) − φ(g)‖₂ − maxᵢ relu(hᵢ(s) − hᵢ(g)).
Iterative "thinking" networks that extrapolate exist, but they read out the final state. Deep Thinking (Schwarzschild et al., NeurIPS 2021; Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2022, with the "recall"/input-injection mechanism), Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., ICLR 2019), and Huginn recurrent-depth (Geiping et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.05171) all iterate a weight-shared block and extrapolate by adding iterations — but the output is decoded from the final hidden state, not summed across steps. Your "recall" re-injection is directly analogous to Bansal et al.'s recall, but they use it to prevent forgetting, not to feed a displacement accumulator.
Per-step displacement norms are used in looped transformers — but only as halting/early-exit triggers, not as the output. This is the closest mechanical neighbor and must be cited and distinguished.
Neural algorithmic reasoning executes Bellman-Ford/Dijkstra and accumulates edge costs, and Value Iteration Networks / Gated Path Planning Networks iterate value iteration — but costs accumulate over an explicit given graph/grid, not over a learned latent trajectory of hidden-state displacements.
The "arc length in latent space" idea is genuinely close but comes from a different community (Riemannian geometry of generative models) and uses curve-optimization, not a recurrent forward pass, and is not goal-conditioned RL.
Details (mechanism-level comparison by search direction)
Direction 1 — Quasimetric / metric goal-conditioned RL
Quasimetric RL (QRL) — Wang, Torralba, Isola, Zhang, ICML 2023 (PMLR pp. 36411–36430), arXiv:2304.01203. (a) Quantity: optimal goal-conditioned value = negative shortest-path quasimetric distance; as OGBench (arXiv:2410.20092) restates the QRL identity, "the shortest path length between two states d*(s,g) is equivalent to the optimal undiscounted goal-conditioned value function... V*(s,g) = −d*(s,g)." (b) Readout: quasimetric distance between two encoder latents (via IQE); the QRL objective maximizes E[d(s,g)] subject to one-step transition (local) constraints. (c) No cross-step accumulation. (d) Goal-conditioned: yes. (e) Local one-step supervision → global distance recovery, philosophically aligned with your short-range-train/long-range-test setup. Closeness: 3/5 — same target quantity (BFS distance) and same "local supervision, global distance" philosophy, but the readout is an embedding quasimetric, not accumulated increments.
Interval Quasimetric Embeddings (IQE) — Wang & Isola, NeurReps 2022, arXiv:2211.15120. (b) d = sum over k components of the Lebesgue measure of a union of intervals [u_ij, max(u_ij,v_ij)]. (c) It sums component-wise interval lengths, which superficially resembles "increment accumulation" — but the sum is over latent dimensions computed in one forward pass, not over iterative refinement steps. Closeness: 2/5 — the "sum of nonnegative local pieces" flavor is present, but there is no iteration and no trajectory. 
[1]
DeepNorm/WideNorm — Pitis, Chan, Jamali, Ba, ICLR 2020 ("An Inductive Bias for Distances: Neural Nets that Respect the Triangle Inequality"). Distance = a learned norm of the difference of embeddings. Closeness: 2/5.
Metric Residual Networks (MRN) — Liu et al. 2023. Distance = symmetric (Euclidean) + asymmetric term between embeddings: V(s,g) = −‖φ(s) − φ(g)‖₂ − maxᵢ relu(hᵢ(s) − hᵢ(g)); a single-shot sum over embedding components, not iterative steps. Closeness: 2/5.
Offline GCRL with Quasimetric Representations (arXiv:2509.20478, 2025) and MetricRL (arXiv:2402.10820): V(s) = γ^{d_Z(φ(s),φ(s_g))}, distance in a static latent. Closeness: 2/5.

Direction 2 — Distance learning in GCRL more broadly
Universal Value Function Approximators (Schaul et al., ICML 2015): scalar value head conditioned on goal. No accumulation. Closeness: 1/5.
Dynamical Distance Learning (Hartikainen et al., arXiv:1907.08225): supervised regression of policy-conditioned distances via an explicit supervised procedure. Scalar head. Closeness: 2/5 (shares distance-regression supervision).
Contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., NeurIPS 2022): value = inner product of representations. Closeness: 2/5.
VIP: Value-Implicit Pre-training (Ma et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.00030): value = negative L2 embedding distance. The paper states verbatim: "we choose the common choice of the negative L2 distance used in prior work Sermanet et al. (2018); Nair et al. (2022): V*(φ(o), φ(g)) := −‖φ(o) − φ(g)‖₂." Closeness: 2/5. R3M (Nair et al., CoRL 2022) and Time-Contrastive Networks (Sermanet et al., ICRA 2018) are the same embedding-norm family.
Horizon Generalization in RL (Myers, Ji, Eysenbach, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2501.02709): formalizes generalizing from short-horizon to long-horizon goals — directly relevant to your extrapolation claim — but achieves it via quasimetric embeddings + planning invariance, not increment accumulation. Closeness: 3/5 on the goal (train short, test long); 1/5 on the mechanism.
Learning Temporal Distances: Contrastive Successor Features (Myers, Zheng, Dragan, Levine, Eysenbach, ICML 2024) and TLDR (Bae, Park, Lee, CoRL 2024): temporal-distance metrics, embedding-based. Closeness: 2/5.
Direction 3 — Iterative networks whose output accumulates over compute steps
Adaptive Computation Time (Graves 2016) and PonderNet (Banino et al. 2021): accumulate halting probabilities and output a probability-weighted mean of per-step predictions. This is a cross-step accumulation, but of halting mass / weighted outputs, not of displacement norms, and it is not a distance. Closeness: 2/5.
Deep Thinking / DT-Recall (Schwarzschild et al., NeurIPS 2021; Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2022): weight-tied, input-injected ("recall") recurrent nets that extrapolate to harder problems with more iterations. Your "recall" re-injection is the same idea (Bansal et al. add recall specifically so the recurrent module retains continuous access to the original input). But output = decode of final state; extrapolation is driven by more iterations, whereas your method fixes T and carries magnitude in the increments. Closeness: 3/5 on architecture (recall + weight-tying + extrapolation); 1/5 on readout. 
[3]
Universal Transformers (Dehghani et al., ICLR 2019), looped transformers, Huginn recurrent-depth (Geiping et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2502.05171): iterate a shared block, read the final state. Closeness: 2/5.
"Two-Scale Latent Dynamics for Recurrent-Depth Transformers" (Pappone, Crisostomi & Rodolà, arXiv:2509.23314, NeurIPS 2025): explicitly computes per-step displacement norms ||Δz|| and consecutive-step angles, and contributes "a second-order exit criterion that halts when consecutive updates stop changing," improving on "the KL-divergence exit strategy of Geiping et al." In other words, displacement is used as a halting signal, not summed into the output. Closeness: 3/5 on the quantity computed; 1/5 because it is a halting signal, not the prediction.
Neural Execution of Graph Algorithms (Veličković et al., ICLR 2020), CLRS-30 benchmark (Veličković et al., ICML 2022): GNNs imitate Bellman-Ford; node distance features accumulate edge costs via min-aggregation over a given graph. Closeness: 2/5 — accumulation is over explicit graph edges, not latent displacements.
Value Iteration Networks (Tamar et al., NeurIPS 2016) and Gated Path Planning Networks (Lee et al., ICML 2018), plus DT-VIN/Highway VIN scaling work: iterate value iteration on a latent MDP; distance emerges from the value map, not from summed hidden-state displacements. Closeness: 2/5.
Direction 4 — Distance as a path integral in a learned latent space
Latent Space Oddity (Arvanitidis et al., ICLR 2018), Riemannian Geometry of Deep Generative Models (Shao et al.), Metrics for Deep Generative Models (Chen et al., AISTATS 2018): geodesic distance = L(γ) = ∫ sqrt(γ̇ᵀ g(γ) γ̇) dt under the decoder pullback metric g = JᵀJ. This is the mathematically nearest idea to "distance = integral of local latent speed." But (i) the distance is obtained by optimizing/solving for a geodesic curve, not by reading off the displacements of a fixed-T recurrent forward pass; (ii) it is unsupervised generative geometry, not BFS-shortest-path-supervised goal-conditioned RL. Closeness: 3/5 conceptually; 1/5 mechanically. 
[4]
OT-Flow (Onken, Wu Fung, Li & Ruthotto, AAAI 2021 vol. 35, pp. 9223–9232, arXiv:2006.00104) and "How to Train Your Neural ODE" (Finlay et al., ICML 2020, arXiv:2002.02798): transport cost T = ∫||v(x,t)||² dt / kinetic energy L_KE = Σ_k ||f(z_k,t_k)||² Δt. In OT-Flow this kinetic-energy/transportation-cost integral is a soft penalty added to the loss "to encourage straight trajectories," not a prediction target. The exact object you accumulate appears here — but as a regularizer, never as the supervised output. Closeness: 3/5 on the quantity; 1/5 because it is auxiliary loss, not output.

Direction 5 — Eikonal / PDE-style neural distance fields
Neural eikonal solvers (Grubas et al. 2022, arXiv:2205.07989), PINNeik (bin Waheed et al. 2021), Riemannian neural eikonal / distance fields in robotics (Li et al., arXiv:2412.05197), Physics-Informed TD Metric Learning (ICLR 2025): a network represents a scalar field u whose gradient norm is constrained (||∇u|| = 1/speed) so u becomes a distance-to-source. Recent work explicitly frames PINN eikonal solving as offline goal-conditioned RL and notes the eikonal equation "defines both an optimal value function and a geodesic distance on a Riemannian manifold." (b) Readout: a scalar field trained via PDE residual, not accumulated increments; distances arise from the PDE constraint, not from summing recurrent displacements. Direction 6 — Cognitive science / neuroscience
Evidence-accumulation / drift-diffusion models: a decision variable integrates noisy evidence until a bound is crossed (variable time). Your method accumulates a nonnegative quantity for a fixed number of steps and the magnitude (not the stopping time) encodes the answer. This is the inverse of DDM's "accumulate to threshold." Closeness: 2/5 — shares "integrate a per-step quantity," but the semantics (fixed-horizon magnitude vs. variable-time-to-bound) differ fundamentally. Successor-representation and mental-simulation/replay accounts of distance judgments estimate distance from replay/simulation length, conceptually adjacent but not a matching neural readout.
Additional directions
Path-integral / linearly-solvable optimal control (Kappen 2005; Todorov 2006): value = −log of a path integral (expectation over trajectories). This is a log-sum-exp over sampled environment trajectories, not a sum of latent hidden-state displacements in a single forward pass. Closeness: 2/5.
Neural A* (Yonetani et al., ICML 2021) and differentiable Dijkstra/Bellman-Ford layers: accumulate edge costs over a given grid/graph; not latent-trajectory arc length. Closeness: 2/5.

Verdict

Three closest works overall:

Quasimetric RL (Wang et al., ICML 2023) + IQE (Wang & Isola 2022) — anticipates: goal-conditioned distance = shortest-path (BFS) quasimetric, local one-step supervision with global distance recovery, and (in IQE) a sum of nonnegative components. Does not anticipate: iterative latent flow, cross-step increment accumulation, goal re-injection/recall, or fixed-T budget-free extrapolation via increment magnitude.
Deep Thinking with Recall (Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2022; Schwarzschild et al., NeurIPS 2021) — anticipates: weight-shared recurrent block, input/goal re-injection ("recall"), and train-easy/test-hard extrapolation. Does not anticipate: increment-sum readout (it decodes the final state), distance-only supervision, or magnitude-carrying-increments-at-fixed-T (their extrapolation needs more iterations).
Riemannian latent geodesics (Arvanitidis et al., ICLR 2018 and successors) / OT-Flow kinetic-energy readout (Onken et al., AAAI 2021) — anticipates: distance/cost as the integral of local latent "speed" (∫||γ̇||). Does not anticipate: producing that length from a fixed-T recurrent forward pass, using it as the supervised output (in OT-Flow it is a regularizer), goal-conditioning on shortest-path labels, or the policy-free-oracle framing.

Components and their status in the literature:

Iterative latent flow toward a goal representation: partially anticipated (Deep Thinking, VINs, Huginn) — but always read out at the end.
Increment-sum readout (Σ||Δz|| as the output): unaccounted for as a supervised distance/value target. Exists only as regularizer (Neural ODE/OT-Flow) / diagnostic (transformer trajectory-geometry probes) / halting signal (looped transformers).
Goal re-injection / recall: anticipated (Bansal et al.).
Budget-free extrapolation with fixed T (magnitude in increments, not step count): unaccounted for — and notably the opposite of the field's usual "more iterations = harder problems" extrapolation (Deep Thinking, Huginn).
Distance-only supervision, no policy (pure oracle): partially anticipated (Dynamical Distance Learning, VIP, temporal-distance methods) — but with embedding-norm/scalar readouts.

Direct novelty assessment: PARTIALLY NOVEL, leaning strongly toward novel on the readout. The target quantity (goal-conditioned BFS distance) and several architectural elements (recall, weight-shared iteration, quasimetric-style local supervision) are well-trodden. But the defining idea — reading out the distance as the accumulated per-step latent displacement norm of a fixed-T recurrent transformer, so that extrapolation beyond the training range comes from increment magnitude rather than step count — appears to have no exact precedent. The nearest mechanical neighbors (looped-transformer displacement-based halting; OT-Flow/Neural-ODE kinetic energy; Riemannian latent geodesics) each use the same mathematical object for a different purpose. You should present the method as novel while explicitly citing and distinguishing these neighbors.
